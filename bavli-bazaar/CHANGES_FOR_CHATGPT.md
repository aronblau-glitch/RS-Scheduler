# Bavli Bazaar: change brief (version 3)

Paste this whole document into ChatGPT (with the original PDF, `Bavli_Bazaar_Print_and_Play_3.pdf`) and ask it to apply the changes to the card game. The "why" next to each change is part of the instructions, so keep the intent even if the exact wording of a card changes.

Version 3 merges two reviews of version 2: the designer's review and a second reviewer's recommendations (called Astra here). Section 3 says what was adopted and what was not.

## 1. What the game is

Bavli Bazaar is a Cover Your Assets-style card game with a Gemara/Talmudic market theme. 3 to 6 players, ages 9+ (the first playtest group is age 8), 20 to 35 minutes, 99 cards. A stated goal of the game is that children learn the real exchange rates between ancient coins.

How it plays (unchanged by this brief):

- Deal 5 cards each. On your turn take one action:
  1. **Match a lot:** pair two matching goods, or a good plus a Wild, or match a good in hand with the top discard.
  2. **Buy from the market:** pay the top discard's exact price with one or two coin cards.
  3. **Challenge a rival:** attack an exposed top lot with a matching good, an exact coin payment, or a Wild.
  4. **Discard** one card.
- Refill to 5 at the end of the turn.
- A challenge: players trade replies (each reply is a new matching good, exact payment, or Wild). The first to pass loses. The winner takes the lot plus every card played.
- **Market Guard** (Shomer) blocks one challenge. The attacker keeps the cards they showed; the defender keeps the lot.
- Fun Mode: your bottom lot is always safe, and the attacker gets one challenge per turn. No Mercy: any exposed top lot is fair game, and a Double Raid is allowed.
- The game ends when the draw pile runs out (finish the trip around the table).
- Hebrew call-outs: Shuka (buying), Meitivei (opening a challenge), Adrabba (replying), Shomer (blocking).

## 2. History: problems found so far

1. Sets were hard to tell apart (several were similar shades of pink, red and orange).
2. In the original deck a Gold Wild was worth 10 points, so adding it to a 1-point Barley made an 11-point lot.
3. The original deck had two numbers on every card (a price and a separate point value) plus a coin-to-point conversion. All the coins together added up to about 8 game points, so they hardly mattered.
4. The deck had "1 Ma'ah" and "3 Ma'ot" coin cards. "3 Ma'ot" is not a real denomination in the money table.
5. The designer wanted a **Penny Jar**-style card (an ancient jar full of perutot) and bigger, motivating numbers like Cover Your Assets.
6. Version 1 copied Cover Your Assets (Gold the top card at 240i), which kept problem 2.
7. Version 2 made Wilds score like their partner and printed real coin values on them. It fixed the swing but made all Wilds play identically, and a Wild with a historical name and a different score could still teach a wrong relationship.
8. Wilds could not be paid for with the coin cards, and the rules did not say whether they can be bought from the discard pile.
9. There was only one Market Guard in 99 cards.

## 3. Review comparison: what was adopted and what was not

| Point | Version 2 | Astra | Version 3 decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wild scoring | Wilds score like their partner (Gold plus Barley = 48i) | Gold 120i, Silver 72i, Jar 24i. Gold plus Barley = 144i | **Adopted Astra's numbers.** Wilds now differ from each other and give a reason to play them. Version 2 made all Wilds identical. |
| Cards in hand | Already stated that they score zero | Stated again, as the reason to play a Wild | **Kept and emphasized.** Hand cards score nothing, so a Wild must be played to count, and covering the lot protects it. |
| Wild names | Gold Dinar, Shekel HaKodesh, with real values printed | Gold Treasure, Silver Treasure, Perutot Jar, so no card claims a coin conversion | **Adopted.** If a Wild scores a game number, its name must not imply a historical coin. |
| Jar value | 12i, then scored as partner | 24i, so the range is 5:1 | **Adopted.** |
| Discard pile rule | Wild, coin or Guard cannot be bought | Only goods may be bought or matched. Wilds, Guards and action cards cannot be taken. | **Adopted**, with coins added to the list. |
| Challenge payment | Exact price of a goods card in the lot | Must equal the printed value of one goods card of the challenged set, not the lot's total | **Adopted**, the clearer wording. |
| Market Guards | 1 | 3 (replace one 2 Zuz and one 4 Zuz coin) | **Adopted.** |
| What a blocked attacker loses | Attacker keeps the shown cards | Discard the Guard and all cards played in that challenge | **Not adopted.** See below. |
| First playtest | Not specified | Base deck, Fun Mode, no expansion | **Adopted.** |

**Why the blocked-attacker rule was not changed.** Astra's wording sends the attacker's shown cards to the spent pile. The current rule lets the attacker keep them. Astra's version is harsher on attackers and, with three Guards in the deck, would discourage challenges. Challenges are the main fun of the game. If the playtest shows Guards are too weak, the rule can be tightened later. The designer should decide this, not ChatGPT.

**Where Astra's numbers are still open to criticism:** Gold plus a Barley is 144i against 48i for two Barley, still a 3:1 swing. It is smaller than version 1's 264i but not removed. Version 2 removed the swing entirely at the cost of making the Wilds dull. Neither is proven. Use Astra's values for the next test, and keep the section 8 fallback in mind.

## 4. The deck (99 cards)

**Principle:** one number per scoring card, in issarin (i). For goods, that number is the price to buy or bid and also the score. For Wilds it is only the score. Coins never score. Anchor: one zuz is 24 issarin. Every goods price can be paid with one or two coin cards.

### Wilds (15 cards) and Market Guard (3 cards)

| Card | Hebrew | Score | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold Treasure | אוצר זהב | 120i | 4 |

All Wilds have equal bidding power and the same simple function (pair with any good, or bid in a challenge). They differ in name, art, score and number of copies. The Jar has no special rule.

Deck total: 56 goods + 25 coins + 15 Wilds + 3 Guards = **99 cards**. Scoring range: 24i to 120i, which is **5:1** (Jar at 24i matches the cheapest goods).

## 5. Rules wording to use

- **Discard pile:** "Only goods may be bought or matched from the top of the discard pile. Wilds, Guards, coins and action cards cannot be taken from it." Wilds are played from hand and are never bought, so no Wild price needs to be payable.
- **Challenge payment:** "An exact coin payment must equal the printed price of one goods card of the challenged set, not the total value of the lot."
- **Scoring:** add the printed issarin on every goods card and Wild in your stack. **Cards left in your hand score nothing**, nor do coins, Guards, spent cards or discards. Highest total wins. Tiebreak: most cards in your stack. Share an exact tie.
- **Coins tucked into a lot as bids score zero.**
- **Market Guard:** play it when someone challenges your lot. The challenge ends immediately and you keep your lot. The Guard goes to the spent pile and scores zero. The attacker keeps the cards they showed.
- Example score: two Wine (48i + 48i) and a Silver Treasure (72i) add up to 168i.
- Example challenge: Esther opens with a matching Olive Oil. Dovid answers with two 1 Zuz coins (24i + 24i), the exact price of one Olive Oil (48i). Esther passes, so Dovid keeps his lot.

## 6. Visual and page changes

- Each of the 9 sets has its own saturated card color: yellow, brown, pink, orange, olive green, purple, blue, red, turquoise.
- Coins are dark charcoal with a cream panel. Wilds are black with a metal ring (bronze for the Jar, silver, gold). The Guard is ivory with a green shield. Expansion action cards are navy.
- Goods and Wilds show their number in a big badge, for example "72i". Goods also show a small "zuz" line.
- The Gold and Silver Treasure and Perutot Jar art are drawn illustrations (pouches and a jar of coins). They are plain placeholders and a real illustration would be better. They carry no coin inscription, so no card appears to name a denomination.
- The "3 Ma'ot" coin card is removed. The 10 and 20 Zuz piles are removed because no price needs them.
- Market guide page: every set with color, price, zuz, how to pay and card count, plus tables for coins, Wilds and Guards. The historical exchange table stays on the reference page and says that the Treasure Wild numbers are game scores, not coin values.
- Print instructions: pages 6 to 27 are the deck, pages 1 to 5 the rules, pages 28 to 30 the optional expansion.

## 7. Optional expansion: Market Day Pack (9 cards, not for the next test)

Three action cards, 3 copies each. Each is played as your one action and then goes to the spent pile. They score zero, cannot be paired and are not bids.

- **Second Look:** put it in the spent pile, draw two, then discard one. Introduce this first.
- **Night Watch:** your exposed top lot cannot be challenged until your next turn. Introduce second.
- **Money Changer:** swap one coin for coins of exactly equal value from the spent pile, picking the replacements before you deposit yours. Introduce last, because it is the most demanding.

**Use the base deck without the expansion for the next test** (Fun Mode, ages 8), so the revised values and challenges can be judged on their own.

## 8. Open questions and untested items

- **Nothing is playtested at these values.** Watch whether Gold plus a Barley (144i against 48i) feels too swingy. **Fallback if it does:** lower the Treasures one step (Gold 96i, Silver 72i), or return to version 2's rule that Wilds score like their partner.
- **Colors:** three sets share each of the 24i and 48i prices, so check that color alone keeps them apart.
- **Hebrew names for the Wilds** (אוצר זהב, אוצר כסף, כד פרוטות) came from the second reviewer and have not been checked by a Hebrew reader. Check them before printing.
- **Teaching the coin values:** the Wilds no longer print historical values. Learning now comes from the exchange table on the reference page and from the Zuz coin cards. If that is too little, add a real-value line to the coin cards, not to the Wilds.
- **Kids' arithmetic:** every price is a multiple of 24. If 8-year-olds struggle, count in zuz (1, 2, 3, 4) and keep issarin as the small line.
- **Bigger numbers (thousands):** considered and rejected, because extra zeros add reading work and no meaning.
- **Cover Your Assets reference:** the table the designer supplied (Gold $50,000, Silver $25,000, top asset $20,000, assets from $5,000 to $15,000) has not been checked against the rulebook. This game deliberately does not copy its Gold-on-top scoring.

## 9. What ChatGPT should preserve

- The Gemara market theme and the Hebrew card names and call-outs.
- The challenge-and-cover mechanic and the two modes (Fun Mode and No Mercy).
- Every goods price payable with one or two coin cards.
- No card whose name or art implies a coin conversion should carry a game score that differs from it.
- Only goods and Wilds score. Guards, coins and hand cards score zero.
