# Bavli Bazaar: change brief

Paste this whole document into ChatGPT (with the original PDF, `Bavli_Bazaar_Print_and_Play_3.pdf`) and ask it to apply the changes to the card game. The "why" next to each change is part of the instructions, so keep the intent even if the exact wording of a card changes.

## 1. What the game is

Bavli Bazaar is a Cover Your Assets-style card game with a Gemara/Talmudic market theme. 3 to 6 players, ages 9+ (the first playtest group is age 8), 20 to 35 minutes, 99 cards.

How it plays (unchanged by this brief):

- Deal 5 cards each. On your turn take one action:
  1. **Match a lot:** pair two matching goods, or a good plus a Wild, or match a good in hand with the top discard.
  2. **Buy from the market:** pay the top discard's exact price with one or two coin cards.
  3. **Challenge a rival:** attack an exposed top lot with a matching good, an exact coin payment, or a Wild.
  4. **Discard** one card.
- Refill to 5 at the end of the turn.
- A challenge: players trade replies (each reply is a new matching good, exact payment, or Wild). The first to pass loses. The winner takes the lot plus every card played.
- **Market Guard** (Shomer) blocks one challenge.
- Fun Mode: your bottom lot is always safe, and the attacker gets one challenge per turn. No Mercy: any exposed top lot is fair game, and a Double Raid is allowed.
- The game ends when the draw pile runs out (finish the trip around the table).
- Hebrew call-outs: Shuka (buying), Meitivei (opening a challenge), Adrabba (replying), Shomer (blocking).

## 2. Feedback from the first playtest (the reasons for the changes)

1. Sets were hard to tell apart (several were similar shades of pink, red and orange).
2. The money made no sense. A Gold Wild was worth 10 points, so adding it to a 1-point Barley made an 11-point lot. Nobody wanted to play a Wild because it was worth more than whole sets.
3. The original deck had two numbers on every card (a price and a separate point value), plus a coin-to-point conversion (1 point per 24 issarin in coins). Across the whole deck the coins added up to only about 8 game points, so they hardly mattered.
4. The deck had "1 Ma'ah" and "3 Ma'ot" coin cards. "3 Ma'ot" is not a real denomination in the game's own money table, so it was confusing.
5. The designer wanted a **Penny Jar**-style card (an ancient jar full of perutot).
6. The designer wanted bigger, motivating numbers like Cover Your Assets, using pictures of coin piles to show larger amounts.
7. A reviewer also said that adding all three expansion actions at once made learning harder, and that some wording was confusing. See section 6.

## 4. The new economy (the main change)

**Principle: one number, in issarin (i), printed big on every card.** For goods, that number is the price (to buy or bid) and also the score. The old separate points and the coin-to-point conversion are removed. **Coins never score.** They are only payment.

Anchor: one zuz is 24 issarin. The five Cover Your Assets price levels ($5k to $25k) map to 24i, 48i, 72i, 96i and 120i, and Gold, the $50k card, maps to 240i. Every price can be paid exactly with one or two coin cards.

Deck total: 56 goods + 27 coins + 15 Wilds + 1 Guard = **99 cards**.

### Why these numbers

- A single number is easier for 8-year-olds than a price plus separate points plus a conversion.
- A 10:1 spread (24i to 240i) copies Cover Your Assets and stays easy to add up. An earlier draft spanned 20:1 (Pearls at 480i) and made one set matter far more than the rest.
- Gold as the top card, with scarce copies, is how Cover Your Assets creates the "worth fighting for" lot.
- The 10 and 20 Zuz coin piles were removed because no price needs them. The pile idea moved onto the Gold and Silver Wild art (the designer's request to use pictures of coin piles to show bigger amounts).
- Real-world lore: the original money table lists Gold Dinar = 600 issarin and Shekel HaKodesh = 96. The game values are scaled for play, not real value, and the rules page says so.

## 5. Visual changes

- Each of the 9 sets has its own saturated card color. The goal is to read the table at a glance. Colors: yellow, brown, pink, orange, olive green, purple, blue, red, turquoise.
- Coins are dark charcoal cards with a cream panel. Wilds are black cards with a metal ring (bronze for Jar, silver for Silver, gold for Gold). The Guard is ivory with a green shield. Expansion action cards are navy.
- The price is printed big in a badge, in issarin (for example "72i"), with a small "zuz" line at the bottom of goods.
- The "3 Ma'ot" coin card is removed.
- New **Perutot Jar** card. The jar is a drawn illustration (plain placeholder). A real illustration would be better.
- The Gold and Silver Wild cards show coin piles instead of a single coin.

## 6. Rules text changes (pages 1 to 5)

- Everywhere "points" appears, use "issarin". "Pay its printed price exactly" replaces "pay its printed fee".
- Scoring: add the printed issarin on every goods card and Wild in your stack. Highest total wins. **Tiebreak: most cards in your stack** (the old tiebreak used leftover coins, which no longer score). Share an exact tie.
- New rule: **coins tucked into a lot as bids score zero.** They only make the loser poorer.
- Example score: two Wine (48i + 48i), a Silver Wild (120i) and a Perutot Jar (12i) add up to 228i.
- Example challenge: Esther opens with a matching Olive Oil. Dovid answers with two 1 Zuz coins (24i + 24i), the exact price of one Olive Oil (48i). Esther passes, so Dovid keeps his lot.
- New "market guide" page: every set with its color, price, zuz, how to pay, and card count, plus tables for coins, Wilds and the Guard.
- Print instructions: pages 6 to 27 are the deck (fronts and backs alternate). Pages 1 to 5 are the rules. Pages 28 to 30 are the optional expansion.

## 7. Optional expansion: Market Day Pack (9 cards, 108 total)

Three action cards, 3 copies each. Each is played as your one action and then goes to the spent pile. Action cards score zero, cannot be paired, and are not bids.

- **Second Look** (start here): put it in the spent pile, draw two, then discard one. You end with the same hand size (5, play it, 4, draw 2, 6, discard 1, 5).
- **Night Watch** (add second): your exposed top lot cannot be challenged until your next turn. In No Mercy it also protects an only lot.
- **Money Changer** (add last): choose one coin in hand. First pick coins from the spent pile with a total exactly equal to it. Then put your coin in the spent pile and take the picked ones. If no combination matches, it cannot be played. Example: one 4 Zuz (96i) for two 2 Zuz (48i + 48i).

Why: a reviewer found three problems in the first draft. Money Changer originally took "newest first", so a player could take their own coins back. Its example needed 4i but only gave up 3i. Night Watch had an unneeded sentence about Market Guard. The reviewer also recommended introducing the cards one at a time. The suggested first game for ages 8+ is **Fun Mode with only the three Second Look cards added (102 cards)**.

## 8. Open questions and things not yet tested

- **Not playtested at the new values.** Watch these in the next test:
  - Gold plus a Barley makes a 264i lot. That matches Cover Your Assets, but if it feels too swingy, lower Gold to 120i first.
  - Three sets share the same price, so check whether the colors alone keep them apart.
- **Market Guard: only 1 copy in 99 cards.** Players will rarely see it. The designer has not decided whether to add more.
- **Perutot Jar value (12i) and count (3)** are guesses, not taken from Cover Your Assets.
- **Kids' arithmetic:** every value is a multiple of 24, which is not round. If 8-year-olds struggle, count in zuz (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10) and keep issarin as the small line. This was suggested but not done.
- **Bigger numbers (the thousands, like $50,000):** considered and rejected for now, because extra zeros add reading work and no meaning. An option that needs no card changes is for the winner to say their total "in thousands" at the end.
- The Cover Your Assets table above was supplied by the designer and not checked against the rulebook.

## 9. What ChatGPT should preserve

- The Gemara market theme and the Hebrew card names and call-outs.
- The challenge-and-cover mechanic and the two modes (Fun Mode and No Mercy).
- Every price stays payable with one or two coin cards.
- Only goods and Wilds score. The Guard and coins score zero.
