# Bavli Bazaar: change brief (version 2)

Paste this whole document into ChatGPT (with the original PDF, `Bavli_Bazaar_Print_and_Play_3.pdf`) and ask it to apply the changes to the card game. The "why" next to each change is part of the instructions, so keep the intent even if the exact wording of a card changes.

Version 2 fixes four problems found in a review of version 1. They are listed in section 2 and resolved in section 5.

## 1. What the game is

Bavli Bazaar is a Cover Your Assets-style card game with a Gemara/Talmudic market theme. 3 to 6 players, ages 9+ (the first playtest group is age 8), 20 to 35 minutes, 99 cards. A stated goal of the game is that children learn the real exchange rates between ancient coins.

How it plays (unchanged by this brief):

- Deal 5 cards each. On your turn take one action:
  1. **Match a lot:** pair two matching goods, or a good plus a Wild, or match a good in hand with the top discard.
  2. **Buy from the market:** pay the top discard's exact price with one or two coin cards.
  3. **Challenge a rival:** attack an exposed top lot with a matching good, an exact coin payment, or a Wild.
  4. **Discard** one card.
- Refill to 5 at the end of the turn.
- A challenge: players trade replies (each reply is a new matching good, exact payment, or Wild). The first to pass loses. The winner takes the lot plus every card played.
- **Market Guard** (Shomer) blocks one challenge.
- Fun Mode: your bottom lot is always safe, and the attacker gets one challenge per turn. No Mercy: any exposed top lot is fair game, and a Double Raid is allowed.
- The game ends when the draw pile runs out (finish the trip around the table).
- Hebrew call-outs: Shuka (buying), Meitivei (opening a challenge), Adrabba (replying), Shomer (blocking).

## 2. Problems to fix

**From the first playtest:**

1. Sets were hard to tell apart (several were similar shades of pink, red and orange).
2. The money made no sense. A Gold Wild was worth 10 points, so adding it to a 1-point Barley made an 11-point lot, and nobody wanted to play a Wild.
3. The original deck had two numbers on every card (a price and a separate point value) plus a coin-to-point conversion. Across the whole deck the coins added up to about 8 game points, so they hardly mattered.
4. The deck had "1 Ma'ah" and "3 Ma'ot" coin cards. "3 Ma'ot" is not a real denomination in the game's own money table.
5. The designer wanted a **Penny Jar**-style card (an ancient jar full of perutot).
6. The designer wanted bigger, motivating numbers like Cover Your Assets, using pictures of coin piles for larger amounts.
7. A reviewer found the three expansion actions confusing and recommended adding them one at a time (section 7).

**From the review of version 1 (all four are fixed here):**

8. Gold plus a Barley was worth 264i against 48i for two Barley. That kept the imbalance from problem 2.
9. Gold Dinar and Shekel HaKodesh had game values (240i and 120i) that differ from their historical values (600i and 96i). Children could learn the wrong relationship between the coins.
10. The brief claimed a 10:1 value range, but with the 12i jar the real range was 20:1.
11. Wilds of 12i and 240i cannot be paid with one or two of the listed coins, and the rules did not say whether Wilds can be bought from the discard pile.

## 3. Reference: Cover Your Assets values, and where this game deliberately differs

The designer supplied this table. It has not been checked against the physical rulebook.

| Card | Value | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Gold wild | $50,000 | 4 |
| Silver wild | $25,000 | 8 |
| Top asset (Home) | $20,000 | 8 |
| Three assets | $15,000 | about 9 each |
| Three assets | $10,000 | about 9 each |
| Three assets | $5,000 | about 10 each |

In Cover Your Assets the Gold wild is the highest-value card. **Bavli Bazaar deliberately does not copy that.** Version 1 did, and it produced problems 8 and 9. Keep the Wild counts (4 Gold, 8 Silver, which also match the original PDF), but do not make Gold the top-scoring card.

## 4. The economy

**Principle: one number per scoring card, in issarin (i).** For goods, that number is the price to buy or bid and also the score. Coins never score. Wilds score like their partner (see below). Anchor: one zuz is 24 issarin. Every goods price can be paid exactly with one or two coin cards.

### Goods (56 cards, 9 sets, each with its own color)

| Set | Hebrew | Color | Price = score | Zuz | Pay with | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barley | שעורים | Yellow | 24i | 1 | 24 | 7 |
| Dates | תמרים | Brown | 24i | 1 | 24 | 7 |
| Wool | צמר | Pink | 24i | 1 | 24 | 7 |
| Pottery | כלי חרס | Orange | 48i | 2 | 48 | 6 |
| Olive Oil | שמן זית | Olive green | 48i | 2 | 48 | 6 |
| Wine | יין | Purple | 48i | 2 | 48 | 6 |
| Fine Linen | פשתן | Blue | 72i | 3 | 24 + 48 | 6 |
| Spices | בשמים | Red | 72i | 3 | 24 + 48 | 6 |
| Pearls | מרגליות | Turquoise | 96i | 4 | 96 | 5 |

The goods range is **4:1** (24i to 96i). The highest possible pair is Pearls at 192i, and the lowest is Barley at 48i. This matches the original deck's 1 to 4 point spread.

### Coins (27 cards, payment only, they never score)

| Coin | Value | Cards |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Zuz | 24i | 9 |
| 2 Zuz | 48i | 9 |
| 4 Zuz | 96i | 9 |

### Wilds (15 cards) and Guard (1 card)

| Card | Real (historical) value, printed on the card | Cards |
|---|---|---|
| Gold Dinar | 25 zuz = 600 issarin | 4 |
| Silver Shekel HaKodesh (sela) | 4 zuz = 96 issarin | 8 |
| Perutot Jar (new) | 96 perutot = 12 issarin | 3 |
| Market Guard (Shomer) | scores 0 | 1 |

**Wilds score the price of the goods in their lot** (a lot always starts with a goods card). A Wild never makes a lot worth more than a plain pair, so Gold plus a Barley is 48i, the same as two Barley. All Wilds have equal bidding power. The three Wilds differ in name, art, real value and number of copies, not in play.

Deck total: 56 goods + 27 coins + 15 Wilds + 1 Guard = **99 cards**.

### Why

- A single number is easier for 8-year-olds than a price plus separate points plus a conversion.
- **Wilds score like their partner** (fixes problems 8 and 10). There is no 264i swing, and the scoring range is just the goods range, 4:1, with no exception for the jar. A Wild is still valuable, because it can pair with any good and bid in any challenge.
- **Wilds print their true historical values** (fixes problem 9). The Gold Dinar shows 25 zuz = 600 issarin and the Shekel HaKodesh shows 4 zuz = 96 issarin, matching the money table on the reference page, so children learn the right relationship. The printed value is for learning and is not the score. The card strip says "Scores as partner" so nobody expects 600 points.
- The Perutot Jar is real value too: 96 perutot make 12 issarin (8 perutot to the issar).
- The 10 and 20 Zuz coin piles were removed because no price needs them. The pile art moved onto the Gold and Silver Wilds.

## 5. Payment and rules clarifications (fixes problem 11)

- **Only goods can be bought.** A Wild, coin or Guard on top of the discard pile cannot be bought or matched. This is why a 12i jar or a Gold Dinar never needs to be paid for, and why the coin set (24, 48, 96) only has to cover goods prices. Wilds are played from hand and never bought.
- **Challenge payment** must be the exact price of a **goods** card in the target lot. A Wild or coin in the lot is never a price to pay.
- **Coins tucked into a lot as bids score zero.** They only leave the loser poorer.
- Scoring: add the printed issarin on every goods card in your stack, plus each Wild scoring the price of the goods in its lot. Highest total wins. **Tiebreak: most cards in your stack.** Share an exact tie.
- Example score: two Wine (48i + 48i) and a Silver Wild stacked with them (it scores 48i) add up to 144i.
- Example challenge: Esther opens with a matching Olive Oil. Dovid answers with two 1 Zuz coins (24i + 24i), the exact price of one Olive Oil (48i). Esther passes, so Dovid keeps his lot.

## 6. Visual and page changes

- Each of the 9 sets has its own saturated card color: yellow, brown, pink, orange, olive green, purple, blue, red, turquoise.
- Coins are dark charcoal cards with a cream panel. Wilds are black cards with a metal ring (bronze, silver, gold). The Guard is ivory with a green shield. Expansion action cards are navy.
- Goods show the price in a big badge, for example "72i", plus a small "zuz" line. **Wilds show a "WILD" badge and their real value** (for example "25 zuz = 600 issarin"), with "Scores as partner" at the bottom.
- The "3 Ma'ot" coin card is removed.
- New **Perutot Jar** card. The jar is a drawn illustration (plain placeholder). A real illustration would be better.
- Gold and Silver show coin piles instead of a single coin.
- New "market guide" page: every set with color, price, zuz, how to pay and card count, plus tables for coins and for Wilds and the Guard (with real values).
- Print instructions: pages 6 to 27 are the deck (fronts and backs alternate). Pages 1 to 5 are the rules. Pages 28 to 30 are the optional expansion.

## 7. Optional expansion: Market Day Pack (9 cards, 108 total)

Three action cards, 3 copies each. Each is played as your one action and then goes to the spent pile. Action cards score zero, cannot be paired and are not bids.

- **Second Look** (start here): put it in the spent pile, draw two, then discard one. You end with the same hand size.
- **Night Watch** (add second): your exposed top lot cannot be challenged until your next turn. In No Mercy it also protects an only lot.
- **Money Changer** (add last): choose one coin in hand. First pick coins from the spent pile with a total exactly equal to it. Then put your coin in the spent pile and take the picked ones. If no combination matches, it cannot be played. Example: one 4 Zuz (96i) for two 2 Zuz (48i + 48i).

Why: the first draft had Money Changer taking "newest first", so a player could take their own coins back, and its example gave up 3i while needing 4i. Night Watch had an unneeded sentence about Market Guard. A reviewer also recommended introducing the cards one at a time. Suggested first game for ages 8+: **Fun Mode with only the three Second Look cards added (102 cards)**.

## 8. Open questions and untested items

- **Not playtested at these values.** Watch whether three sets sharing each of the 24i and 48i prices stay distinguishable by color alone.
- **Wilds all play the same.** Gold, Silver and the Jar differ only in art, real value and count. If the playtest finds that dull, the next step is to give the Wilds different effects, not different scores. Do not give them back large scores.
- **Cover Your Assets feel.** The designer asked for it, and this version departs from it for balance and for historical accuracy. If Wilds feel too weak, one option is a small flat bonus (for example +24i for a Gold), but test that against problem 8 before using it.
- **Market Guard: only 1 copy in 99 cards.** The designer has not decided whether to add more.
- **Kids' arithmetic:** every price is a multiple of 24. If 8-year-olds struggle, count in zuz (1, 2, 3, 4) and keep issarin as the small line.
- **Bigger numbers (thousands):** considered and rejected, because extra zeros add reading work and no meaning.
- **The 4 Zuz coin is the same value as a Shekel HaKodesh (a sela).** The coin card is a plain stack and the Wild shows the historical coin. If it confuses players, rename the coin card "Sela".
- The Cover Your Assets table was supplied by the designer and not checked against the rulebook.

## 9. What ChatGPT should preserve

- The Gemara market theme and the Hebrew card names and call-outs.
- The challenge-and-cover mechanic and the two modes (Fun Mode and No Mercy).
- Every goods price payable with one or two coin cards.
- Printed historical values that match the money table. Never print a game score as if it were a coin's value.
- Only goods and Wilds score. The Guard and coins score zero.
